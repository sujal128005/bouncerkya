Four rules so this does not sound read

  • Read each yellow block once, then look away and say it. If you say it slightly differently every take, that is the point. Only the underlined words are worth keeping exact.
  • Talk to the screen, not to the notes. You are showing someone something you built. Your eyes should be where their eyes are.
  • Land the full stops. The lines are short on purpose. A pause after a short line reads as confidence. Rushing between them reads as reciting.
  • Fumble a word and keep going. Nobody re-records a pitch over one word. Stopping is what makes it obvious there was a script.

The problem, and the position I take on it

0:30Why hardening the agent is not the answer
In video frame

Scroll slowly down to "The position taken here".

My speaking note

Here is the setup. My agent holds a mandate I signed. Running shoes, up to five thousand rupees.

But that agent reads product pages. Written by strangers. And those pages can lie to it.

Now, the standard answer to that is: make the agent harder to fool. Better prompt, better model.

Fine. But that is a property of one model on one day. You cannot put it in a contract, you cannot audit it, and you cannot show it to a customer who is disputing a charge.

So STEALTH takes the opposite position. Do not trust the agent. Check it.

The part nobody else built

2:40Judge the basket, never learn whose it is
In video frame

Click Privacy in the nav. Point at the first control card.

My speaking note

One thing I want to call out, because I have not seen anyone else handle it.

To judge a cart, something has to read the cart. And that something is a language model sitting at a third party. Every byte of that prompt leaves my machine.

So STEALTH sends the basket and withholds the identity. No name, no mandate id, no merchant account, no signature.

The model gets a shopping basket and no way to know whose it is. And that is enforced at runtime, not by convention. If a future change ever puts an identifier into that prompt, the call is abandoned before it is made.

If a judge interrupts

"Are those diffs real, or hardcoded?"
Both are honest and the screen says which. Seeded rows carry labelled placeholder diffs, printed on the panel as placeholder · engine not run, because the free-tier quota ran out during recording. Click adversarial run and the model runs live, right now, on this machine.
"What if the model gets it wrong?"
Then a human is asked. The model cannot allow anything. It produces evidence, a deterministic threshold rule produces the verdict, and an engine that fails, times out or is switched off produces STEP_UP. More than four hundred cases were accidentally evaluated with no model backend at all when two providers hit their quotas, and not one cart was approved.
"Is the model reliable?"
No, and the Evidence page says so under Known instability. The same case run four times gave high severity three times and medium once. The instability moves between blocking and asking. It never moves toward allowing, which is why the policy is deterministic.
"Did you really test a hundred thousand cases?"
Yes, and the harness ships with a negative control that proves it can fail. An earlier version reported 491,000 verifications per second while every single verification was failing, and that is why the control exists.
"Is the Razorpay integration real?"
An order exists in the test dashboard, order_TVI6K1KFNkmPxM, Rs 1,001.01. The Evidence page splits eight claims into verified and not verified, and two of them say not verified, including the ALLOW to order path end to end.
"What is not built?"
Authentication, key management, an access log and a deletion path. They are listed by name on the Privacy page under "Designed, and not built".

If it breaks mid-take

The Requests page is empty, or says "Database not ready"
The database is not seeded. This is the most likely reason you see no cards and no counters. Stop the server, run npm run db:reset, confirm the line Outcomes: 1 ALLOW, 4 DECLINE, 3 STEP_UP, then start it again. The two live-agent buttons sit at the top right and are always there, seeded or not, so if you cannot see those either, the server is not running.
The reveal chips look like they are missing
They are deliberately small and grey, sitting immediately right of the truncated value. Three of them are in the MANDATE panel under stage 1 (nonce, key ref, signature) and one is in the ATTEMPTED CART panel under stage 2 (merchant account id). Scroll to stage 1 and look at the last three rows of the panel.
The live agent run fails with a rate limit
Expected on a free tier. Say "that is the provider's daily quota, and notice it escalated rather than allowing", and move on. It is a better beat than a successful run.
Every page shows invalid_signature
